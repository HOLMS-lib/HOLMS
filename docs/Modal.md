# Syntax and semantics of modal logic in HOLMS

This chapter presents modal syntax, Kripke semantics, and their basic results
in ordinary mathematical language. Each result includes a statement and an
informal proof; no knowledge of a proof assistant is assumed.

See the [mathematical documentation index](README.md) for other chapters.
The formal developments are [HOL Light's `modal.ml`](../modal.ml) and
[Lean's `HOLMS/Modal.lean`](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean). References below identify
corresponding declarations or clauses, rather than replacing mathematical
statements. Lean names are relative to the namespace `HOLMS`.
Implementation choices are discussed in the
[Modal translation notes](../lean/translation/Modal.md).
The [obsolete Italian version](Modal.it.obsolete.md) is retained for historical
reference and is no longer maintained.

## 1. Modal formulas

Let $A$ be a nonempty countable set of propositional variable names. Both
formalizations use strings for these names. Write $\mathcal F$ for the set of
finite formulas generated by

$$
p ::= \bot \mid \top \mid a \mid \neg p \mid (p\land p)
  \mid (p\lor p) \mid (p\to p) \mid (p\leftrightarrow p) \mid \Box p,
\qquad a\in A.
$$

An atom is identified in notation with its name $a$. The connectives in this
grammar are primitive syntactic constructors, including the propositional
connectives. Define two abbreviations:

$$
\Diamond p := \neg\Box\neg p,
\qquad
\boxdot p := \Box p\land p.
$$

The first expresses possibility, dual to necessity; the second combines the
necessity of $p$ with $p$ itself. Neither adds a constructor to the grammar.
Throughout the chapter, $u$ denotes either unary constructor $\neg,\Box$, and
$\circ$ denotes any of $\land,\lor,\to,\leftrightarrow$. Statements using
these parameters apply separately to every indicated constructor.

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `form_INDUCT`,
`form_RECURSION`, `diam_DEF`, `dotbox_DEF`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form`, `Form.diam`, `Form.dotbox`.

### 1.1 Unique syntactic construction

**Statement.** Every formula has exactly one outermost constructor. Each
constructor is injective in its arguments, and distinct constructors have
disjoint images. In particular,

$$
u(p)=u(q)\iff p=q,
\qquad
(p\circ q)=(r\circ s)\iff(p=r\ \land\ q=s),
$$

and $\Box p\ne(q\land r)$ for all $p,q,r$; atom construction is injective
in the variable name as well.

**Comment.** These are syntactic equalities, not logical equivalences. They
allow case analysis on the shape of a formula.

**Proof.** Regard a formula as a finite rooted tree labelled by its
constructors and atomic names. Equal trees have equal root labels and equal
ordered lists of children. Conversely, equal labels and children give the
same tree. The grammar supplies all possible root labels. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `form_CASES`,
`form_DISTINCT`, `form_INJ`. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): the cases and
constructor injectivity/disjointness principles generated for `Form`; there
are no separately written HOLMS theorems matching these three HOL Light names.

### 1.2 Terminal constituents and depth

Let $T(p)$ be the set of terminal constituents of $p$, ignoring repeated
occurrences. For a leaf $\ell\in\{\bot,\top\}\cup A$, define

$$
T(\ell)=\{\ell\},\qquad T(u(p))=T(p),\qquad
T(p\circ q)=T(p)\cup T(q).
$$

Thus terminal constituents include constants as well as atoms. Define the
syntactic depth $d(p)$ by

$$
d(\ell)=0,\qquad d(u(p))=d(p)+1,\qquad
d(p\circ q)=1+\max\{d(p),d(q)\}.
$$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `atomicals` is an
OCaml traversal of formula terms; depth is a local witness in `COUNTABLE_FORM`,
not a global definition. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.atomicals`
and `Form.depth`.

### 1.3 Countability of formulas

**Statement.** If $A$ is countable, then

$$
|\mathcal F|\le |\mathbb N|.
$$

**Comment.** Formulas are finite trees over countably many labels. The bound
on depth makes that observation into an induction argument.

**Proof.** Put $U_n=\{p\in\mathcal F:d(p)<n\}$. We prove by induction on
$n$ that $U_n$ is countable. The base case is $U_0=\varnothing$. A formula
in $U_{n+1}$ is a constant, an atom, a unary constructor applied to a member
of $U_n$, or a binary constructor applied to two members of $U_n$. Constants
and atoms form a countable set. By the induction hypothesis, so do the unary
images of $U_n$ and the binary images of $U_n\times U_n$. There are only
finitely many constructors, so their union is countable. Every formula has
finite depth and belongs to $U_{d(p)+1}$. Hence
$\mathcal F=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb N}U_n$ is countable. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `COUNTABLE_FORM`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.countable`, using the countability
instance derived for `Form`. The formal statements concern string-labelled
formulas; the argument above explains the more general countable-alphabet fact.

## 2. Immediate constituents and subformulas

Write $p\prec q$ when $p$ is an **immediate constituent** of $q$. The defining
rules are

$$
p\prec u(p),\qquad p\prec(p\circ q),\qquad q\prec(p\circ q).
$$

Write $p\preceq q$ when $p$ is a **subformula** of $q$: this is the reflexive
and transitive closure of $\prec$. Equivalently, there is a finite path of
immediate-constituent steps from $p$ to $q$, possibly of length zero.

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `IN_MINOR_RULES`
(defining `MINOR`) and `SUBFORMULA`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.Minor`, `Form.Subformula`.

### 2.1 Inversion for immediate constituents

**Statement.** For every formula $p$, leaf $\ell$, and formulas $q,r$,

$$
\neg(p\prec\ell),\qquad
p\prec u(q)\iff p=q,\qquad
p\prec(q\circ r)\iff(p=q\ \lor\ p=r).
$$

**Comment.** A leaf has no immediate constituents; a unary formula has its
argument; a binary formula has its two arguments, possibly equal.

**Proof.** Inspect the defining rules for $\prec$. No rule ends in a leaf.
Exactly one rule ends in a given unary constructor, and exactly two end in a
given binary constructor. Constructor injectivity identifies their arguments;
disjointness excludes all other rules. Conversely, each indicated equality
allows the corresponding defining rule to be applied. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): the nine clauses of
`MINOR_CLAUSES`. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.not_minor_falsum`, `Form.not_minor_verum`, `Form.not_minor_atom`,
`Form.minor_neg_iff`, `Form.minor_box_iff`, `Form.minor_conj_iff`,
`Form.minor_disj_iff`, `Form.minor_imp_iff`, `Form.minor_iff_iff`.

### 2.2 Reflexivity and inclusion of immediate constituents

**Statement.** For all formulas $p,q$,

$$
p\preceq p,\qquad p\prec q\ \Longrightarrow\ p\preceq q.
$$

**Proof.** The empty path witnesses the first assertion. Appending the given
immediate step to the empty path at $p$ witnesses the second. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `SUBFORMULA_REFL`;
the second assertion follows from it and `SUBFORMULA_TRANS_L`, with no
separately named theorem. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.Subformula.refl`, `Form.Minor.subformula`.

### 2.3 Transitivity and extension by one step

**Statement.** For all formulas $p,q,r$,

$$
(p\preceq q\ \land\ q\preceq r)\Longrightarrow p\preceq r,
$$

$$
(p\preceq q\ \land\ q\prec r)\Longrightarrow p\preceq r,
\qquad
(p\prec q\ \land\ q\preceq r)\Longrightarrow p\preceq r.
$$

**Comment.** Subformula paths can be concatenated, including when either
path consists of a single immediate step.

**Proof.** For transitivity, induct on the path from $q$ to $r$. If it is
empty, use $p\preceq q$. If it ends in an immediate step, the induction
hypothesis joins the first path to the preceding part of the second; append
the final step. The second assertion simply appends a step. For the third,
turn $p\prec q$ into $p\preceq q$ by Section 2.2 and use transitivity.
$\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `SUBFORMULA_TRANS`,
`SUBFORMULA_TRANS_L`, `SUBFORMULA_TRANS_R`, respectively.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.Subformula.trans`,
`Form.Subformula.trans_minor`, `Form.Minor.trans_subformula`.

### 2.4 Decomposition at the last or first step

**Statement.** For all formulas $p,q$,

$$
p\preceq q\iff
p=q\ \lor\ \exists r\,(p\preceq r\ \land\ r\prec q),
$$

$$
p\preceq q\iff
p=q\ \lor\ \exists r\,(p\prec r\ \land\ r\preceq q).
$$

**Comment.** A subformula path is empty or has a last step; equivalently, it
is empty or has a first step. The last-step form drives constructor inversion.

**Proof.** The first equivalence follows by inspecting whether the path is
empty or ends in an immediate step. For the second, induct on the path. An
empty path gives equality. When a step is appended to an empty path, that
step is the first step and the remaining path is empty. When a step is
appended to a nonempty path, retain its first step and append the new step
to its remaining path. The converses follow from reflexivity and the
one-step extension rules of Section 2.3. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `SUBFORMULA_CASES_L`
and `SUBFORMULA_CASES_R`. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.subformula_cases_tail_iff`, `Form.subformula_cases_head_iff`.

### 2.5 Inversion for subformulas

**Statement.** For every formula $p$, leaf $\ell$, and formulas $q,r$,

$$
p\preceq\ell\iff p=\ell,
$$

$$
p\preceq u(q)\iff(p=u(q)\ \lor\ p\preceq q),
$$

$$
p\preceq(q\circ r)\iff
(p=(q\circ r)\ \lor\ p\preceq q\ \lor\ p\preceq r).
$$

**Comment.** These equations recursively describe all subformulas, including
the formula itself, and will justify their finite enumeration.

**Proof.** Apply last-step decomposition. For a leaf there is no possible
last immediate step, so only equality remains. For a unary formula the last
step must come from its argument. For a binary formula it must come from
one of its two arguments. Section 2.1 identifies these possibilities, giving
the displayed alternatives. Conversely, equality gives reflexivity, and a
subformula of an argument extends by its immediate step into the compound
formula. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): the nine clauses of
`SUBFORMULA_INVERSION`. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.subformula_falsum_iff`, `Form.subformula_verum_iff`,
`Form.subformula_atom_iff`, `Form.subformula_neg_iff`, `Form.subformula_box_iff`,
`Form.subformula_conj_iff`, `Form.subformula_disj_iff`,
`Form.subformula_imp_iff`, `Form.subformula_iff_iff`.

### 2.6 Descending through a compound subformula

**Statement.** For all formulas $p,p_1,p_2,q$,

$$
u(p)\preceq q\Longrightarrow p\preceq q,
$$

$$
(p_1\circ p_2)\preceq q\Longrightarrow p_1\preceq q,
\qquad
(p_1\circ p_2)\preceq q\Longrightarrow p_2\preceq q.
$$

**Comment.** Once a compound formula occurs inside $q$, each of its arguments
also occurs inside $q$.

**Proof.** Each argument is an immediate constituent of the compound formula.
Prepend that immediate step to the given subformula path, using the third
rule of Section 2.3. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): the ten clauses of
`MINOR_SUBFORMULA`. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.of_subformula_neg`, `Form.of_subformula_box`,
`Form.of_subformula_conj_left`, `Form.of_subformula_conj_right`,
`Form.of_subformula_disj_left`, `Form.of_subformula_disj_right`,
`Form.of_subformula_imp_left`, `Form.of_subformula_imp_right`,
`Form.of_subformula_iff_left`, `Form.of_subformula_iff_right`.

## 3. Finite enumeration of subformulas

Define a finite set $E(p)$ recursively by

$$
E(\ell)=\{\ell\},\qquad
E(u(p))=\{u(p)\}\cup E(p),
$$

$$
E(p\circ q)=\{p\circ q\}\cup E(p)\cup E(q).
$$

These clauses use only singletons and finite unions. Write
$\operatorname{Sub}(p)=\{q\in\mathcal F:q\preceq p\}$ for the set defined
by the subformula relation.

**Formalization references.** [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.subformulas`. [HOL Light](../modal.ml) has no separately named recursive
enumeration $E$; `FINITE_SUBFORMULA` reasons directly about $\operatorname{Sub}$.

### 3.1 Correctness of the enumeration

**Statement.** For all formulas $p,q$,

$$
q\in E(p)\iff q\preceq p.
$$

**Proof.** Induct on the structure of $p$, with $q$ arbitrary. For a leaf,
membership means equality with that leaf, exactly as in Section 2.5. For
$u(r)$, membership means $q=u(r)$ or $q\in E(r)$. The induction hypothesis
turns the second alternative into $q\preceq r$, and unary inversion gives
the result. For $r\circ s$, membership means equality with the compound
formula or membership in $E(r)$ or $E(s)$. Apply the two induction hypotheses
and binary inversion. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`Form.mem_subformulas_iff`. There is no separately named counterpart in
[HOL Light](../modal.ml); the same set decomposition is used in the proof
of `FINITE_SUBFORMULA`.

### 3.2 Finiteness of subformulas

**Statement.** For every formula $p$,

$$
|\operatorname{Sub}(p)|<\infty.
$$

**Proof.** Section 3.1 identifies $\operatorname{Sub}(p)$ with $E(p)$.
The recursive construction of $E(p)$ is finite: structural induction starts
with a singleton and preserves finiteness by adjoining one element and taking
finite unions. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `FINITE_SUBFORMULA`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.finite_subformulas`.

### 3.3 Finitely many sets of subformulas and their negations

**Statement.** For every formula $p$, put

$$
C(p)=\operatorname{Sub}(p)\cup\{\neg q:q\in\operatorname{Sub}(p)\}.
$$

Then

$$
|\{X\subseteq\mathcal F:X\subseteq C(p)\}|<\infty.
$$

**Comment.** This supplies a finite collection of candidate sets for later
canonical-model and decision constructions. Here $C(p)$ adds one negation
of each subformula; it does not mean closure under iterated negation.

**Proof.** The subformula set is finite by Section 3.2, and its image under
negation is finite as well. Their union $C(p)$ is therefore finite. The
collection in the statement is the power set of $C(p)$, and the power set
of a finite set is finite. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml):
`FINITE_SUBSET_SUBFORMULAS_LEMMA`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.finite_subsets_subformulas`.

### 3.4 Enumeration without repetitions

**Statement.** For every formula $p$, there is a finite list
$L=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)$ such that

$$
\bigl(\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ q_i=q_j\Rightarrow i=j\bigr)
\quad\land\quad
\bigl(\forall q\in\mathcal F,\ q\in L\iff q\preceq p\bigr).
$$

**Proof.** Enumerate the finite set $\operatorname{Sub}(p)$ once per element.
The resulting list has no repeated entries and contains exactly the desired
formulas. Equivalently, enumerate $E(p)$ and use Section 3.1. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `SUBFORMULA_LIST`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.subformula_list`.

## 4. Kripke semantics

Fix an ambient domain $D$. A **frame** is a pair $F=(W,R)$ with
$W\subseteq D$ and $R\subseteq D\times D$. Members of $W$ are its designated
worlds. At this level we impose neither nonemptiness of $W$ nor a requirement
that $R$ stay inside $W\times W$.

A **valuation** is a function $V:A\times D\to\{\mathrm{false},\mathrm{true}\}$.
A **model** is a triple $M=(W,R,V)$. Satisfaction $M,w\models p$, also written
$F,V,w\models p$, is defined at every $w\in D$, even outside $W$:

$$
M,w\not\models\bot,\qquad M,w\models\top,\qquad
M,w\models a\iff V(a,w),
$$

$$
M,w\models\neg p\iff\neg(M,w\models p),
$$

$$
M,w\models p\land q\iff(M,w\models p\ \land\ M,w\models q),
$$

$$
M,w\models p\lor q\iff(M,w\models p\ \lor\ M,w\models q),
$$

$$
M,w\models p\to q\iff(M,w\models p\ \Longrightarrow\ M,w\models q),
$$

$$
M,w\models p\leftrightarrow q\iff(M,w\models p\ \Longleftrightarrow\ M,w\models q),
$$

$$
M,w\models\Box p\iff
\forall v\in W,\ R(w,v)\Longrightarrow M,v\models p.
$$

The propositional clauses have their classical meaning. The modal clause
only inspects accessible successors belonging to $W$.

A formula is **valid in a frame**, written $F\models p$, when it holds for
every valuation at every designated world. It is **valid in a class of
frames** $\mathcal L$ when

$$
\mathcal L\models p\iff\forall F\in\mathcal L,\ F\models p.
$$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `holds`, `holds_in`,
`valid`, `WORLDS`, `ACCREL`. [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Frame`,
`Valuation`, `Model`, `Form.holds`, `Form.holdsIn`, `Form.Valid`.

### 4.1 Characterization of frame validity

**Statement.** For every frame $F=(W,R)$ and formula $p$,

$$
F\models p\iff\forall V\,\forall w\in W,\ F,V,w\models p.
$$

**Comment.** Unlike satisfaction, validity quantifies only over designated
worlds. Thus a frame with $W=\varnothing$ validates every formula.

**Proof.** The equivalence unfolds the definition of frame validity. When
$W$ is empty there are no worlds at which the condition must be checked.
$\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `HOLDS_IN`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.holdsIn_iff`.

### 4.2 Formula interpretations range over all predicates

For a fixed frame $F$ on $D$, let $\llbracket p\rrbracket_{F,V}$ denote the
predicate $w\mapsto(F,V,w\models p)$ on the entire ambient domain $D$.

**Statement.** For every property $P$ of predicates on $D$,

$$
\bigl(\forall p\in\mathcal F\,\forall V,\ P(\llbracket p\rrbracket_{F,V})\bigr)
\iff
\bigl(\forall U:D\to\{\mathrm{false},\mathrm{true}\},\ P(U)\bigr).
$$

**Comment.** Valuations are unrestricted, so an atom can realize any desired
predicate. This is useful when translating modal validity schemes into
conditions on the accessibility relation.

**Proof.** For the forward direction, take an arbitrary predicate $U$.
Choose an atom $a_0$ (possible since $A$ is nonempty), and set $V(a,w)=U(w)$
for every $a,w$. The atomic satisfaction clause gives
$\llbracket a_0\rrbracket_{F,V}=U$, so the premise yields $P(U)$. Conversely,
if $P$ holds for every predicate, apply it to the interpretation of each
formula under each valuation. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `HOLDS_FORALL_LEMMA`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.holds_forall_iff`. Both use a string
atom, so the required nonempty alphabet is available.

## 5. Bisimulation

Let $M_i=(W_i,R_i,V_i)$ be models on ambient domains $D_i$, for $i=1,2$.
A relation $Z\subseteq D_1\times D_2$ is a **bisimulation** if, whenever
$w_1Zw_2$, all of the following hold:

1. **Membership:** $w_1\in W_1$ and $w_2\in W_2$.
2. **Atomic agreement:** $V_1(a,w_1)\iff V_2(a,w_2)$ for every $a\in A$.
3. **Forth:** for every $u_1\in D_1$ with $R_1(w_1,u_1)$, there is
   $u_2\in W_2$ with $R_2(w_2,u_2)$ and $u_1Zu_2$.
4. **Back:** for every $u_2\in D_2$ with $R_2(w_2,u_2)$, there is
   $u_1\in W_1$ with $R_1(w_1,u_1)$ and $u_1Zu_2$.

The forth and back clauses quantify over **all ambient successors**, not
only designated ones. Membership then forces matched successors to lie in
their respective world sets. This is the convention in both formalizations;
no global restriction on $R_i$ has been assumed.

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `BISIMIMULATION`
(the spelling is that of the source). [Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean):
`BisimulationAt` contains the clauses at a pair of points; `Bisimulation`
requires them at every related pair.

### 5.1 Bisimulation invariance

**Statement.** If $Z$ is a bisimulation between $M_1$ and $M_2$, then for
every formula $p$ and all $w_1\in D_1,w_2\in D_2$,

$$
w_1Zw_2\Longrightarrow
\bigl(M_1,w_1\models p\iff M_2,w_2\models p\bigr).
$$

**Comment.** Modal formulas cannot distinguish bisimulation-related worlds.
This is the key ingredient in the validity transfer results below.

**Proof.** Fix the models and the bisimulation. Induct on the structure of
$p$, proving the assertion for **all pairs of related worlds** simultaneously.
This generality allows the induction hypothesis to be used at successors.

For $\bot$ and $\top$, the assertion follows from their semantics. For an
atom, it is the atomic agreement condition. Each propositional connective
preserves the equivalence given by the induction hypotheses. For example,
a conjunction holds just when both conjuncts hold, and each conjunct has
the same truth value at the two related worlds. Negation and the other
binary connectives follow in the same way.

For $p=\Box r$, suppose $M_1,w_1\models\Box r$. Take any $u_2\in W_2$
with $R_2(w_2,u_2)$. The **back** clause supplies $u_1\in W_1$ with
$R_1(w_1,u_1)$ and $u_1Zu_2$. The assumption at $w_1$ gives
$M_1,u_1\models r$; the induction hypothesis at the related successors
then gives $M_2,u_2\models r$. Since $u_2$ was arbitrary,
$M_2,w_2\models\Box r$. The converse uses **forth** in the same manner.
$\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `BISIMIMULATION_HOLDS`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Bisimulation.holds_iff`.

### 5.2 Bisimilar pointed models have designated points

Define **bisimilarity** of pointed models by

$$
(M_1,w_1)\sim(M_2,w_2)\iff
\exists Z\,(Z\text{ is a bisimulation between }M_1,M_2\ \land\ w_1Zw_2).
$$

**Statement.**

$$
(M_1,w_1)\sim(M_2,w_2)\Longrightarrow(w_1\in W_1\ \land\ w_2\in W_2).
$$

**Proof.** Choose a witnessing bisimulation. Its membership clause at the
related pair gives both inclusions. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `BISIMILAR`
(definition), `BISIMILAR_IN` (statement).
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Bisimilar`, `Bisimilar.mem_worlds`.

### 5.3 Bisimilarity preserves truth

**Statement.** For every formula $p$,

$$
(M_1,w_1)\sim(M_2,w_2)\Longrightarrow
\bigl(M_1,w_1\models p\iff M_2,w_2\models p\bigr).
$$

**Proof.** Choose the bisimulation witnessing bisimilarity, and apply
Section 5.1 to the related pair. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `BISIMILAR_HOLDS`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Bisimilar.holds_iff`.

## 6. Transfer of validity

### 6.1 Transfer between two frames

**Statement.** Let $F_i=(W_i,R_i)$ be frames on $D_i$. Suppose

$$
\forall V_1\,\forall w_1\in D_1\,\exists V_2\,\exists w_2\in D_2,
\quad ((F_1,V_1),w_1)\sim((F_2,V_2),w_2).
$$

Then, for every formula $p$,

$$
F_2\models p\Longrightarrow F_1\models p.
$$

**Comment.** Validity transfers from the target frame back to the source
frame. The target valuation and point may depend on both the source valuation
and the source point.

**Proof.** Assume $F_2\models p$. Fix any valuation $V_1$ and designated
world $w_1\in W_1$. The hypothesis supplies $V_2,w_2$ and bisimilar pointed
models. Section 5.2 gives $w_2\in W_2$, so validity on $F_2$ implies
$F_2,V_2,w_2\models p$. Section 5.3 transfers this truth to
$F_1,V_1,w_1$. Since $V_1$ and $w_1\in W_1$ were arbitrary, $F_1\models p$.
$\square$

**Note on the hypothesis.** Both formal statements quantify over all
$w_1\in D_1$, without a premise $w_1\in W_1$. This is stronger than the
hypothesis needed by the proof: it even forces $W_1=D_1$ by Section 5.2
(apply the hypothesis with any valuation). The same argument proves the
useful weaker-premise version in which only $w_1\in W_1$ must be matched.
That version is not the literal statement of the declarations cited here;
it also follows from the next theorem by taking singleton frame classes.

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `BISIMILAR_HOLDS_IN`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.holdsIn_of_bisimilar`. Their
hypotheses agree, including the quantification over the entire ambient domain.

### 6.2 Transfer between classes of frames

**Statement.** Let $\mathcal L_i$ be classes of frames on $D_i$. Suppose

$$
\forall F_1=(W_1,R_1)\in\mathcal L_1\,\forall V_1\,\forall w_1\in W_1,
\quad\exists F_2\in\mathcal L_2\,\exists V_2\,\exists w_2\in D_2,
\quad ((F_1,V_1),w_1)\sim((F_2,V_2),w_2).
$$

Then, for every formula $p$,

$$
\mathcal L_2\models p\Longrightarrow\mathcal L_1\models p.
$$

**Comment.** Unlike the preceding formal theorem, this hypothesis requires
matches only for designated source worlds. The target frame may vary with
the source frame, valuation, and point.

**Proof.** Assume $\mathcal L_2\models p$. Choose $F_1\in\mathcal L_1$,
a valuation $V_1$, and $w_1\in W_1$. By hypothesis there are
$F_2\in\mathcal L_2$, $V_2$, and $w_2$ giving a bisimilar pointed model.
Section 5.2 ensures $w_2\in W_2$. Class validity gives $F_2\models p$,
so $F_2,V_2,w_2\models p$. By Section 5.3, $F_1,V_1,w_1\models p$.
Arbitrariness of the frame, valuation, and designated source world yields
$\mathcal L_1\models p$. $\square$

**Formalization references.** [HOL Light](../modal.ml): `BISIMILAR_VALID`.
[Lean](../lean/HOLMS/Modal.lean): `Form.valid_of_bisimilar`.

## 7. Role in the wider development

Countability and finite subformula enumeration provide the syntactic basis
for later model constructions. Kripke semantics connects formulas with
frames and valuations. Bisimulation invariance and validity transfer allow
models to be transported while preserving the modal truths of interest.
Together these results support the subsequent developments of correspondence,
soundness, completeness, decidability, and countermodel construction.
